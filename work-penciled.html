Penciled - Designing proactive scheduling for healthcare clinics

Overview

An AI agent that fills cancelled appointments - automatically

Penciled's AI agent detects cancelled and open appointments, then reaches out to waitlisted patients through voice and text to fill those slots - giving clinics operational visibility and control throughout the process. When I joined, the core product needed a rethink: the existing workflow was reactive, filling only post-cancellation slots while leaving already-open provider availability entirely unmanaged.

My work focused on redesigning the scheduling workflow around proactive capacity recovery - turning what was a manual, repetitive task for clinic staff into a single configurable automation.

Cover - Penciled AI scheduling platform, calendar + fill run view
Problem

Clinics were losing revenue to a problem they couldn't see

Healthcare clinics carry recurring provider availability every week - time that goes unfilled not because there's no demand, but because the operational overhead of finding and contacting the right patients manually is too high. Staff were recreating the same workflows week after week, and the tools weren't keeping up.

"We found ourselves recreating the same fill workflows every week just to recover recurring provider availability."
- Clinic operator feedback

↗
Unmet operational need
Operators were manually creating fill runs to recover already-open provider capacity - a repeated, time-intensive task that had no automation path.
↗
Open availability was invisible
Provider schedules contained recurring open slots that staff struggled to fill. Without visibility into fillable slots, time simply went unused.
↗
Existing systems were reactive
Recovery only triggered after a cancellation. Already-open provider availability was a separate problem entirely - and no system addressed it.
How might we
Help clinics fill recurring open provider slots without repetitive manual effort?

The reframe mattered: the core problem wasn't a lack of automation - it was the repetition of a task. Clinic staff already knew how to fill slots; they just had to do it over and over with no memory or system behind it.

Process

Operational research, workflow mapping, then design

The solution evolved through close collaboration with clinic operators and engineers. Before touching screens, I needed to understand the actual scheduling ecosystem - how providers, appointments, waitlists, and outreach connected in practice.

01

Interviewed clinic operators and power users to map pain points, workarounds, and unmet needs in daily scheduling workflows.
02
Insight
Identified that repetition - not complexity - was the root issue. Clinics didn't need to learn a new system; they needed to stop rebuilding the same one.
03
Ideate
Ran collaborative whiteboarding sessions to define relationships between providers, appointments, waitlists, and AI-generated fill runs before designing flows.
04

Built and tested interactive prototypes with clinic staff, iterating on the fill run creation flow and AI-generated slot review experience.

Defining Success

What success should look like

Before designing anything, I worked with the team to define what success meant for both the business and the users. This alignment ensured that every design decision had a clear connection to measurable outcomes.

What success should look like
1. Faster slot recovery
Reduce the time required to identify and fill recurring open provider availability.

2. Reduced manual scheduling effort
Decrease manual effort involved in creating and managing fill workflows.

Metrics to track

Design principles

Four principles to guide every decision

01
Invisible but accessible
Automatic filling should remain accessible without disrupting existing scheduling workflows. The AI operates in the background.
02
Context aware
The system should understand provider availability, scheduling constraints, and operational context before generating any actions.
03
Human in the loop
Automation should accelerate workflows, not replace human oversight. Operators retain control at every decision point.
04
Operationally reliable
Scheduling actions and bookings must remain synchronised, predictable, and accurate across the entire clinic ecosystem.
Solutions

Redesigned around proactive capacity recovery, not reactive triage

The core shift was architectural: instead of responding to cancellations after they happened, the platform now proactively surfaces and fills recurring open provider capacity - with AI doing the outreach and humans staying in control of approvals.

Unified calendar view
Provider schedules and open availability were surfaced in a single calendar view, giving clinic staff full operational visibility for the first time. Open slots were distinguishable from booked ones at a glance.
Fill run creation
A simple modal flow let operators configure a fill run - appointment type, duration, location, scheduling constraints - and trigger the AI agent to start outreach. What previously took repeated manual steps now took under a minute.
AI-proposed slots
The AI agent scanned open slots, generated appointment propositions, and surfaced them for human review directly within the calendar. Operators could approve, modify, or remove each proposed slot before outreach began.
Live run tracking
Approved fill runs became live and trackable inside the scheduling view. Clinics could monitor outreach progress, slot status, and booking activity in real time - without leaving the calendar.
Manual override
Operators could create additional manual runs, cancel existing ones, or modify individual slots at any point - the automation accelerated the workflow but never locked them out of it.

AI UX

Making AI activity visible, traceable, and controllable

One of the central design challenges was trust. Clinic operators needed to understand what the AI was doing, why, and be able to intervene at any moment. The design solved this through transparency at every stage of the automated workflow.

↗
Proposals, not actions
The AI generated slot proposals for human review, not direct bookings. Every outreach action required explicit operator approval before patients were contacted.
↗
Status at a glance
Fill runs showed live status - Ongoing, Proposed, Filled - directly on the calendar, so operators always knew what was in progress without needing to dig into logs.
↗
Non-disruptive integration
AI-generated runs sat inside the same calendar view as manually-created appointments. There was no separate "AI mode" - automation was embedded in the existing workflow.
Fill run active state - AI outreach in progress, slot statuses visible in calendar

Reflection

What I learned

Reframe before you design
The biggest design decision wasn't a UI choice - it was reframing the problem from "add automation" to "eliminate repetition." That shift changed everything about the solution.
Trust is the hardest UX problem in AI
Getting clinic staff to trust an AI acting on their behalf required making every AI action visible, reversible, and explainable. Transparency wasn't a feature - it was the product.
Embed in the existing workflow
Placing AI activity inside the existing calendar - rather than a separate AI view - was the decision that made adoption fast. People trust tools that fit how they already work.
Talk to operators, not just end users
The most important insights came from clinic managers and front desk staff - the people with the actual operational pain - not just from high-level product goals. Proximity to the problem matters.
