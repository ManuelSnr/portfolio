Hyper — Designing a social gaming platform for the US market

Led end-to-end design of the v2 platform for Metaverse Magna, enabling a successful iOS App Store launch, US market expansion, and a dramatic reduction in KYC friction and support costs.

Overview

A real-money gaming platform that needed to earn trust fast

Hyper is a skill-based 1v1 duel and tournament platform where players compete using in-game currency with real monetary value. When I joined, the product needed a full v2 redesign to support a US market launch on the iOS App Store — and the existing KYC and payout flows were generating a high volume of support tickets and costly manual verification.

My remit was broad: redesign the core app experience, own the design-to-engineering handoff, and fix the compliance flows that were bleeding time and money.

Problem

Trust gaps at every critical moment

Users were dropping off at three points: onboarding (unclear value proposition), KYC verification (confusing instructions and opaque status), and cash-out (lack of confidence in the process). Each failure had a direct cost — either in lost users or in manual support intervention.

The existing design had grown organically and lacked a coherent system. There were inconsistent components, no documented patterns, and no clear handoff process between design and engineering — making every iteration slow.

  • High drop-off at KYC verification step
  • Support team overwhelmed with "why is my withdrawal pending?" tickets
  • No design system — inconsistent UI patterns across the app
  • No PRD process — misaligned expectations between design and engineering

Approach

System-first, then screens

Before touching the UI, I audited every existing screen and mapped the full user journey — identifying every trust gap, every unclear state, and every place users were likely to stall. I ran a short workshop with the PM and founders to align on priorities.

I then built a foundational design system — colour tokens, typography scale, component library — that would make every future iteration faster and more consistent. This became the basis for the first company PRD, giving engineering a clear, structured handoff document for the first time.

  • Audited all existing screens and mapped end-to-end user journey
  • Ran prioritisation workshop with PM and founders
  • Built the company's first Figma design system and component library
  • Authored the first PRD — covering flows, states, edge cases, and handoff specs
  • Redesigned onboarding, game flow, KYC, and cash-out in parallel sprints

Key Work — Compliance UX

Making KYC feel less like a checkpoint, more like a handshake

The biggest lever was the KYC and cash-out flow redesign. Users were confused about what was needed, why it was needed, and what "pending" meant. I redesigned the flow with three principles: clarity at every step (what you need to do and why), visible progress (where you are in the process), and immediate feedback (what happens next).

I also added a proactive education layer — short inline explanations of why verification is required — which reduced "why do you need my ID?" support tickets significantly.

Reflection

What I learned

This project reinforced how much leverage a designer has when they work on the system, not just the screen. The PRD and design system I built didn't just make this launch better — they changed how the whole team works.

Designing for real-money mechanics also deepened my understanding of trust UX: users aren't just evaluating whether a product is usable, they're evaluating whether they can trust it with their money. Every loading state, every status message, every micro-copy choice is a trust signal.
