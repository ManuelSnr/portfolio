Overview

A real-money gaming platform that needed to earn trust fast

Hyper is a skill-based casual gaming platform where users compete in 1v1 duels, enter tournaments, and challenge friends — with in-game currency that carries real monetary value. Despite MVM raising $3.2M in funding, the product faced a critical problem: an APK-only distribution model that excluded iOS users entirely, created legitimacy concerns before users ever experienced the product, and generated a D7 retention rate below 11%.

My remit was broad — redesign the v2 platform to support an iOS App Store launch, US market expansion, and a compliant real-money gaming experience that users would actually trust.

Cover image — Hyper v2 platform overview
Problem

A fragmented experience built on broken trust

The APK-only experience created friction at every critical moment. Downloading a file flagged as potentially harmful by Android, moving through an inconsistent sign-up flow, then landing in a dashboard with no clear next step — each of these was a trust failure. Data showed that users who made it through were almost exclusively referred by friends.

"The website was Hyper's primary acquisition channel, but downloading the APK introduced multiple trust and onboarding barriers before users could even experience the product."

Three structural problems compounded each other:

↗
Not scalable
APK-only distribution excluded iOS entirely and limited Android reach due to gambling-related Play Store restrictions.
↗
Not sustainable
Pre-value friction weakened onboarding completion. Only 10% of users who completed sign-up purchased HPR — Hyper's in-game currency.
↗
Not compliant
Expanding into the US market introduced stricter expectations around legitimacy, platform trust, and real-money gaming compliance.
↗
Retention was broken
Less than 11% of users became activated players — defined as someone who plays 8 games in 7 days — with no clear retention loops post-onboarding.

Research

Breaking down the experience to find the real problem

I started with a full onboarding journey teardown using UXCam and Hotjar analytics, mapped against qualitative survey data from users. The goal was to find exactly where trust broke down and why.

Journey teardown
Identified trust gaps, friction points, and drop-offs screen by screen. Annotated every confusing interaction and ambiguous button behaviour across the full onboarding flow.
User surveys
Collected qualitative insights from users to understand hesitation around downloads and top-ups. Users described the platform as feeling like a "Ponzi scheme" before referrals or visible payout proof changed their perception.
Behavioural data
Users with HPR already in their wallet were significantly more likely to return and continue playing. Referral code steps increased drop-off. Post-onboarding relied on self-discovery with no guiding system.
Insights synthesis
Combined data sources to identify three core patterns: trust must be earned before monetisation is introduced; the referral step was anti-momentum; and the platform lacked any progression system to bring users back.

Design principles

Three principles to guide every decision

01
Reward effort
Reinforce user actions with visible progress and meaningful rewards that encourage continued engagement. Every action should feel like it counts.
02
Build trust, form habits
Trust is earned through clear next steps and transparent systems. Habits form when players have a reason to return that isn't just monetary.
03
Design for clarity and momentum
Every screen should answer three questions: what do I do next, why does it matter, and how close am I to progress.
Solutions

Turning insights into flows that convert

The redesign rebuilt the platform around intent, progression, and engagement — rather than rushing users toward monetisation. Each solution mapped directly to a research finding.

  • 01
    Visible winnings before sign-up
    The welcome screen showed real player wins and contextualised HPR earnings in local currency — giving new users social proof before any commitment. User testing showed this meaningfully reduced initial scepticism.
  • 02
    Goal-setting as a commitment device
    Instead of asking for immediate top-up, onboarding now asked players to set a daily earning target. This reframed the platform as a performance tool and created a psychological anchor for return behaviour.
  • 03
    Gameplay before monetisation
    Players were directed to their first free game before any reward or top-up prompt. The four games with the highest play rates were surfaced first. Experience of value preceded the ask.
  • 04
    Streak system for daily retention
    Inspired by Duolingo's streak mechanics, a lightweight daily play streak introduced soft commitment without financial pressure. "I'm committed" CTA wording reinforced player identity.
  • 05
    Contextual education over upfront friction
    HPR explanation was moved inline to moments of hesitation, not front-loaded. A dedicated "Not sure yet?" FAQ page addressed top-up concerns without blocking the main conversion path.
  • 06
    Referral step relocated
    Data showed the referral code step causing momentum loss during sign-up. It was moved post-onboarding, removing a friction point at the most sensitive stage of the flow.

Compliance UX

Protecting the platform without breaking momentum

Expanding into the US market required careful compliance design. Real-money gaming is not permitted in all US states, and KYC verification was generating significant cost and user frustration through failed attempts.

↗
Location-gated access
Age and location checks were introduced before enabling top-ups. Users in restricted states could still access solo games and free friend battles.
↗
KYC guidance before submission
Most failed verifications were caused by poor lighting, cropped IDs, or unclear photos. Pre-submission guidance reduced these failures. Two free attempts were introduced to manage cost.
↗
Duplicate KYC reduction
Duplicate account creation was tied to account recovery attempts. An early recovery path ("Do you have a verified Hyper account?") reduced redundant verifications.
↗
Wait-state management
Average verification time was 3 minutes. A countdown progress indicator was added so users could either wait or safely navigate home without abandoning the process.
KYC flow — ID selection, photo guidance, verification progress
Retention loops

Closing the loop — giving players reasons to return

Onboarding was never the finish line. After players completed the initial flow, the experience needed systems that would pull them back. Three loops were designed to work together.

Discovery feed
A swipe-based game discovery feed helped surface games quickly and sustain attention, modelled on short-form content behaviour. Game trailers were shown only for the selected game to optimise performance.
Daily goals
Dashboard-level daily goal tracking gave players visible progress toward their self-set earning target, anchoring return behaviour to personal intent rather than external incentives.
Live tournaments
Large multiplayer tournaments ensured users had HPR in their balance from winnings, creating natural repeat-play cycles. Live tournament banners surfaced time-sensitivity to drive engagement.
Post-onboarding experience — dashboard, daily goal, live tournaments
Design system

Structure before pixels — building the foundation

Before shipping screens, I built a Figma design system that gave the team a shared language for the first time. Typography scale (Instrument Sans for UI, Founder Grotesk for emphasis), a full colour system including grey contrast ratios, button hierarchy with bonus-state variants, and detailed handoff documentation with numbered UI breakdowns for every screen.

The system helped the team move faster and keep the experience consistent across platforms and sprints.

Reflection

What I learned

Trust before monetisation
Introducing value before the ask — gameplay before top-up, social proof before sign-up — was the single most impactful structural change. Users convert when they believe first.
Compliance as UX
KYC and age verification are often treated as legal checkboxes. Approaching them as designed flows — with guidance, progress states, and recovery paths — meaningfully reduced both cost and friction.
Retention is a design problem
Streaks, daily goals, and tournament loops weren't features bolted on — they were the product. Retention needs to be designed into the core experience, not added after conversion.
Systems multiply impact
The design system and PRD process I built didn't just improve this launch — they changed how the whole team works. Investing in the foundation pays compounding returns.
